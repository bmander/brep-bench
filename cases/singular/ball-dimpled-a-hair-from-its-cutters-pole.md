A ball of radius 10 centered on the origin, less a ball of radius 0.8 whose center is 10.4 from
the origin, at height z = 5.501, on the half-plane that leaves the z axis at 120° from +x toward
+y: the center (ρ cos 120°, ρ sin 120°, 5.501), ρ = √(10.4² − 5.501²) ≈ 8.826. One dimple, as on a
golf ball.

The spheres meet in a circle square to the line through their centers: it cuts a cap 3/130 high
from the big ball and leaves inside it a cap 49/130 high of the small one, which is the dimple's
floor.

This case is about that circle passing a hair from a pole. If the balls you make have poles, put
each ball's on the vertical through its center. The small ball's lower pole, (ρ cos 120°,
ρ sin 120°, 4.701), then lies 0.00008 inside the big ball, so the circle passes about 0.00009 from
it. (With the center at height 5.5 it would pass through the pole.)
