A ball of radius 5 centered on the origin, with a hole of radius 2 bored right through it along the
z axis. The bore meets the ball in two circles of radius 2, at z = ±√21. Chamfer the upper one,
at z = √21 ≈ 4.583, with two distances: 2 down the bore and 1 on the ball.

Each distance is measured in a straight line from the edge, in the plane through the z axis (the
plane square to the edge): the chamfer meets the bore on the circle at z = √21 − 2, and the ball
on the circle whose points are 1 in a straight line from the rim, the chord 1 long, turned
2·asin(1/10) about the ball's center away from the bore. That circle has radius
ρ₁ = 49/25 + 3√231/50 ≈ 2.872 and lies at z₁ = 49√21/50 − 3√11/25 ≈ 4.093. The chamfer is the cone
between the two circles, and the solid is the bored ball cut by it. The lower rim stays sharp.

(Measured along the ball as an arc, 1 would stop about 1/600 short of that circle: the straight
line is what is meant.)
