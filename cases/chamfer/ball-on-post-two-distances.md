A post, the cylinder of radius 3 on the z axis from z = 0 to z = 10, joined to a ball of radius 5
centered at (0, 0, 10). The post's side meets the ball in the circle of radius 3 at z = 6, a
concave edge. Chamfer that edge with two distances: 2 down the post and 1 on the ball. A concave
chamfer adds material: it fills the groove between the post and the ball.

Each distance is measured in a straight line from the edge, in the plane through the z axis (the
plane square to the edge): the chamfer meets the post on the circle at z = 4, and the ball on the
circle whose points are 1 in a straight line from the edge, the chord 1 long, turned 2·asin(1/10)
about the ball's center away from the post. That circle has radius ρ₁ = 147/50 + 6√11/25 ≈ 3.736
and lies at z₁ = 152/25 + 9√11/50 ≈ 6.677. The chamfer is the cone between the two circles, and the
solid is the post and the ball joined, with the ring between them and the cone filled in.

(Measured along the ball as an arc, 1 would stop about 1/600 short of that circle: the straight
line is what is meant.)
