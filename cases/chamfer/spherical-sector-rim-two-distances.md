A spherical sector: the part of a ball of radius 5 centered on the origin that lies inside the cone
with its apex at the origin, about the z axis, opening upward with half angle α, cos α = 3/5 (about
53.13°). The cone's side meets the ball in the circle of radius 4 at z = 3, the sector's rim.
Chamfer the rim with two distances: 2 down the cone and 1 on the ball.

Each distance is measured in a straight line from the edge, in the plane through the z axis (the
plane square to the edge): the chamfer meets the cone on the circle 2 down its side from the rim,
of radius 12/5 at z = 9/5, and the ball on the circle whose points are 1 in a straight line from
the rim, the chord 1 long, turned 2·asin(1/10) about the ball's center up from the rim. That circle
has radius ρ₁ = 98/25 − 9√11/50 ≈ 3.323 and lies at z₁ = 147/50 + 6√11/25 ≈ 3.736. The chamfer is the
cone between the two circles, and the solid is the sector cut by it.

(Measured along the ball as an arc, 1 would stop about 1/600 short of that circle: the straight
line is what is meant.)
