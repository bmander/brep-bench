The lens common to two balls of radius 5, one centered at (0, 0, 3) and one at (0, 0, −3). Its
upper face is part of the ball about (0, 0, −3) and its lower face part of the ball about
(0, 0, 3); they meet in its rim, the circle of radius 4 at z = 0. Chamfer the rim with two
distances: 2 on the upper face and 1 on the lower.

Each distance is measured in a straight line from the edge, in the plane through the z axis (the
plane square to the edge): the chamfer meets each face on the circle whose points are that far in
a straight line from the rim, the chord turned 2·asin(d/10) about that face's ball's center. On the
upper face that circle has radius ρ₂ = 92/25 − 12√6/25 ≈ 2.504 and lies at
z₂ = 16√6/25 − 6/25 ≈ 1.328; on the lower face, radius ρ₁ = 98/25 − 9√11/50 ≈ 3.323 at
z₁ = 3/50 − 6√11/25 ≈ −0.736. The chamfer is the cone between the two circles, and the solid is
the lens cut by it.

(Measured along a ball as an arc, d would stop about d³/600 short of its circle: the straight line
is what is meant.)
