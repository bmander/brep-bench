The part of a ball of radius 5 centered on the origin that lies below the plane z = 3: a ball cut
flat above its middle. Its flat face is the disk of radius 4 at z = 3, and it meets the ball in
that disk's rim at an angle of about 126.9°, not square. Chamfer the rim with two distances: 2 on
the flat face and 1 on the ball.

Each distance is measured in a straight line from the edge, in the plane through the z axis (the
plane square to the edge): the chamfer meets the flat face on the circle of radius 2, and the ball
on the circle whose points are 1 in a straight line from the rim, the chord 1 long, turned
2·asin(1/10) about the ball's center down from the rim. That circle has radius
ρ₁ = 98/25 + 9√11/50 ≈ 4.517 and lies at z₁ = 147/50 − 6√11/25 ≈ 2.144. The chamfer is the cone
between the two circles, and the solid is the cut ball cut again by it.

(Measured along the ball as an arc, 1 would stop about 1/600 short of that circle: the straight
line is what is meant.)
