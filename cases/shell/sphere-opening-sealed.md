The part of the ball of radius 5 centred on the origin below the plane z = 4.5. Shell it inward to a
wall 1 thick, open at its flat face, z = 4.5. The outside of the wall is its sphere, unmoved, and
the inside is the sphere moved 1 inward along its normal, onto the surface offset 1 from it. The
moved faces are extended or trimmed where they meet one another, and run on until they meet the
surface of an opening, which is left as a rim between the outside and the inside.

The flat face is the disc of radius √4.75 at z = 4.5. The sphere, moved 1 inward, is the sphere of
radius 4 about the origin, which lies wholly below the plane: it never meets it, so the cavity
cannot reach the opening and no rim is made. The flat face stays as it was, every point of it within
1 of the sphere, and the cavity is the closed ball of radius 4: the result is the part of the ball
of radius 5 below z = 4.5 less the ball of radius 4, one solid bounded by two shells. A kernel that
cannot tell that the opening is never reached may refuse; one that removes the flat face anyway, or
opens a hole in it, is wrong.
