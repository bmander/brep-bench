A solid cone on the z axis, its base the disc of radius 6 at z = 0 and its apex at (0, 0, 8), so its
side is 10 long from the base to the apex and its half-angle α has sin α = 3/5. Shell it inward to a
wall 1 thick, open at its base, z = 0. The outside of the wall is its side, unmoved, and the inside
is the side moved 1 inward along its normal, onto the surface offset 1 from it. The moved faces are
extended or trimmed where they meet one another, and run on until they meet the surface of an
opening, which is left as a rim between the outside and the inside.

The side, moved 1 inward, is the cone with the same half-angle whose apex is 1/sin α = 5/3 lower, at
(0, 0, 19/3); it meets the plane z = 0 in the circle of radius 19/4. The apex, a point where the
side has no normal, moves along the axis. The result is the cone less that cone, its rim the annulus
between radii 19/4 and 6 at z = 0.
